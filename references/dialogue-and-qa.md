# Dialogue and Q&A

## Speaker roles

Assign stable speaker IDs first, then infer conversational functions such as host/questioner, primary respondent, secondary respondent, expert, narrator, or participant. A person may change function within one exchange, so store both identity and per-unit function.

Do not infer a questioner from punctuation alone. Use voice continuity, turn-taking, address terms, answer dependency, topic control, and sustained behavior.

## Q&A units

For each question identify:

- necessary setup;
- the shortest formulation that makes the answer understandable;
- direct answer or conclusion;
- reasoning, example, qualification, and follow-up;
- where the next question actually begins.

## Short-video semantic roles

Use the following role vocabulary when grouping concise, publishable units. A group may carry one primary role and optional secondary roles, but role labels must describe the communication function rather than the visual treatment:

`hook`, `background`, `question`, `reaction`, `answer`, `evidence`, `technical_detail`, `contrast`, `benefit`, `summary`, `cta`.

Every highlight group records:

- stable semantic-unit IDs and the final subtitle references that express them;
- the preceding/following context needed to understand the group;
- speaker ID and per-unit function;
- why the group earns short-video emphasis;
- whether it contains a number, model, brand, unit, condition, negation, comparison, or technical fact;
- whether it requires human listening before any downstream packaging.

The role is an editorial handoff only. It does not choose a flower-text template, position, motion, or sound.

A host's long framing may contain the guest's eventual answer. When the guest states the same point fully, retain the actual question and prefer the guest's complete expression. Preserve host context that the answer otherwise lacks.

## Multiple respondents

If respondents repeat the same information, keep the clearer complete version or combine the first person's core point with only the second person's new contribution. Do not retain duplicate content merely to balance screen time unless representation is an explicit requirement.

## Interruptions and overlap

Classify overlap by function:

- assent only;
- completion of another person's sentence;
- correction;
- new evidence;
- topic redirection;
- competitive interruption.

Preserve the channel carrying the core information. Remove assent-only overlap when it can be cut cleanly. Keep correction or new evidence and verify intelligibility from actual audio, not transcript order.

## Continuity audit

After cuts, verify that no answer begins without a recoverable question, no follow-up appears before its premise, speaker references remain clear, and an edit has not made one person appear to agree with a different statement.
