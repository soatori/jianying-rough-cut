# Speech cleanup and repeated attempts

## Fillers and discourse markers

Pure hesitation sounds are strong candidates, but no token is an unconditional blacklist. Common words such as “then,” “so,” “actually,” “right,” “well,” “this,” or their equivalents can carry structure, stance, response, reference, or emotion.

Delete a filler only when its semantic contribution is empty and the resulting audio remains natural. If removing the word produces a hard splice, retain it or shorten only the adjacent pause.

## Repeated attempts

1. Confirm that several attempts express the same intended idea.
2. Identify the complete version, including any necessary subject, setup, transition, qualifier, or conclusion.
3. Remove only abandoned, wrong, dangling, or fully covered material.
4. Prefer the version that is complete, accurate, natural, and well connected; do not mechanically choose the last take.

Keep one natural structural lead-in when several restarts repeat it. Do not remove every connector and leave an abrupt content fragment.

## False starts and stutters

Remove a fragment when it is clearly abandoned and contributes no useful information. For local stutters or repeated words, cut at word level only when both consonant/vowel boundaries and surrounding prosody permit it. If a local cut cannot sound natural, keep the phrase or choose a larger complete retake boundary.

## Self-correction

Protect corrections involving facts, names, numbers, units, polarity, or conditions. Usually keep the corrected complete statement and remove the erroneous attempt, but retain context that the corrected phrase depends on.

## Pass separation

Do not mix uncertain micro-cleanup into Pass 1. Content structure should be auditable without dozens of word-level cuts. Pass 2 may contain aggressive candidates, but unresolved joins stay marked for listening rather than execution.
